## Game Balancers' Job is not to Balance the Game

it's to make the game fun. Multiplayer games.
- you can make the game perfectly balanced by making everything the same
- the perception of balance is actually more important 
- the value of games at a high level is the fun, and if you perceive somethign to be unfair, then you're losing value in the game
- StarCraft 2 balance changes have been horrible because they've lost the VISION of the game. you need to balance to support the player fantasy. sc2 unit size changes are an example of a horrible change that's meant solely for balance without any support for player fantasy.
- Note that some games are explicitely unbalanced, and that's actually okay. i.e. chess, go, turn based games. 
- A note on balancing on statistics: it's kidn of inevitable when your game hits a certain scale, you have to start implementing some sort of statistics. I think this isn't necessarily bad. If your players get ahold of the numbers though, it might make your life more annoying if they keep pointing to numbers that have little meaning. 
- "Placebo Buffs": I think this is something that's well understood, that you can buff a number for a champion, and it will make players feel better. Sometimes the buff just doesn't actually have to be meaningful. But player perception is in fact, the most important thing. This is because it's actually pretty common for there to be hidden strategies that are never found by players at all, or much later into a metagame that are known by the developers. So even though the developer knows more objetively what is strong and what is not strong, they may choose to balance what the players see as strong and what is not strong. This is balancing against what is objectively balanced, but it still might make sense. 
- Balancing across skill levels. It's important to realize that 99.99% of your player base is not going to be professional level, or even close to that level. So the thing that's going to retain them and keep playing is if the game is awesome and fun. Yes, you should consider professional play and ensure that the game is interesting to watch at a high level, but that should just be one of the _many_ considerations you should have. I certainly believe that balancing around the .01% of players is certainly a mistake in most instances. I'm not saying you shouldn't nerf busted strategies, you should. But my understanding is that professional players A. have a vested interest in your balance decisions B. They will likely focus on what happens when you take a strategy to very limit of power possible. So it's not _really_ balancing much, and affects little players.
